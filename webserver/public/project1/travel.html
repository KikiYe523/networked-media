The Photo Diary

2024—2026

These are not complete records
of everywhere I went

only the moments I remembered to photograph
and the details I did not want to lose

Open a date to read an entry.



August 2024 — Qinghai

I remember the altitude before anything else.
The air was thin, the roads kept climbing,
and prayer flags moved above us.

I tried to photograph the scale of everything,
but the mountains always continued
beyond the frame.


September 2024 — New York

When I first arrived, I photographed everything:
buildings, strangers, signs,
and light falling between the streets.

I was still looking at New York as a visitor.
I did not know it would slowly become home.


March 2025 — Orlando

It was warmer here.
For a few days, the only plan
was deciding what to do next.

I remember long afternoons,
bright colors, and not needing
to think very far ahead.


July 2025 — Beijing & Jiangsu

This trip felt like moving
between different speeds.

Beijing was crowded streets and old walls.
Jiangsu was gardens, water,
and towns reflected back at themselves.

I liked seeing ordinary life continue
inside places carrying so much history.


August 2025 — France

I spent a lot of this trip looking upward:
at windows, stone walls,
church ceilings, and unfamiliar streets.

There always seemed to be music nearby.
I walked slowly because every few steps
there was something else I wanted to photograph.


January 2026 — Western & Central U.S.

Most of this trip happened through a window.
The landscape changed from snow to stone
and then into long stretches of cityscape.

I photographed places where we stopped,
but also the places we simply passed through.


March 2026 — Philadelphia

I was only here briefly.
A few streets, unfamiliar buildings,
and the last light over the city.

Then the train left,
and the photographs became
most of what I remembered.


June 2026 — Shenzhen & Hong Kong

These places are familiar to me,
but they never look exactly the same
when I return.

Old buildings disappear,
new ones rise beside them,
and daily life continues through both.

Photographing home makes me notice
how much changed while I was away.


August 2026 — Xinjiang

The landscape felt wider
than anything my camera could hold.

There were horses moving through water,
mountains remaining at the horizon,
and roads continuing between them.

I kept taking photographs anyway,
knowing each one could preserve
only a small part of being there.
